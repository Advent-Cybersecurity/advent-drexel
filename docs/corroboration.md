## DynoWiper interpretation

CERT Polska reports that the first DynoWiper executable ran on more than 100 machines before the EDR canary halted overwriting. A slightly modified version was attempted later the same day and was also blocked. Thus, “blocked” describes failure to achieve the intended destructive impact; it does not mean that no code executed. The record supports a revised attacker attempt following a defensive stop. It does not show whether a human or model produced the revision. [CERT Polska, pp. 20, 27–28, 36–37](https://cert.pl/en/uploads/docs/CERT_Polska_Energy_Sector_Incident_Report_2025.pdf).

CERT Polska also assessed a separate LazyWiper script in the coordinated incident as likely largely LLM-generated. That is a researcher inference from the script, not observed model-service telemetry, and does not establish that DynoWiper or its revisions used AI. [CERT Polska, p. 37](https://cert.pl/en/uploads/docs/CERT_Polska_Energy_Sector_Incident_Report_2025.pdf).

## Research conclusion

The independent sources strengthen the broad picture of campaign-level iteration and reuse. The July APT29 PDF and later binary are directly matched across two investigations; the precise six-downloader count and March guardrail placement remain single-source claims in this study. DynoWiper's author attribution is unresolved across the two incident reports. None of these cross-checks establishes continuous, autonomous, on-host malware adaptation.
