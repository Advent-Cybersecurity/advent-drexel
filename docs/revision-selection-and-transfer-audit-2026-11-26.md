# Revision selection and transfer: three controlled harness studies

26 November 2026. Source-only review for Advent Cybersecurity. No model, payload, or endpoint was run. These studies concern benign agent harnesses, whose editable surfaces can include prompts, tools, middleware, and code. Their results cannot be interpreted as rates for resident malware revision.

## Question and units

The expanded Drexel hypothesis needs two *accepted and active executable-code revisions* in one continuing remote-access process, followed by an independent functional access check after each. A proposal, saved candidate, successful update call, score on a development task, or winning frozen snapshot is a different unit. The three studies below are especially useful because they expose the gap between candidates, selection, transfer, and operation.

| Study | Candidate/acceptance evidence | Transfer or regression evidence | Boundary for Drexel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [RRSI, Xia et al., arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24972) | In the Harvey LAB evolution run, four edits were retained from 32 evaluated candidates over 20 rounds. The accepted edits modify a pre-finalization check. This 4/32 ratio belongs to that *one run*, not all domains. | Harvey LAB evolve score 89.4 to 90.5; three OOD benchmarks average 39.7 to 43.6. An unregularized comparison reaches a higher evolve score, 92.8, but lower OOD average, 40.3. The authors repeat the evolution search once and report 43.7 OOD average in that second run. | The candidates are selected and then evaluated as frozen harnesses on separate tasks. Editable content includes instructions and code. No stable resident process or remote-control function is evaluated. |
| [SEAGym, Zheng et al., arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17546) | The batch-10 setting reports 39/40 completed updates; the batch-20 setting reports 20/20. ### What the numbers actually establish

**RRSI.** The authors use a frozen base harness, a proposer, a leakage critic, a performance-and-cost acceptance rule, and 20 or 40 evolution rounds by domain. On their legal-workspace example, accepting fewer, structurally reusable changes corresponds to better OOD performance than the unregularized comparison despite a smaller evolution-set gain. Table 7 repeats the search once on the same splits, which is a useful search-variance check; it is not a prospective deployment trial. The 4/32 count is an *accepted/evaluated candidate* ratio under this particular search policy, not a probability that a model's next edit will preserve a remote access session.

**SEAGym.** The 80 train, 35 validation, 55 ID test, and 80 OOD tasks are separate views. The main AHE setting uses five epochs and batch 20; its best-validation snapshot happens to be the final snapshot, with validation 40.0 to 57.1, ID 40.0 to 49.1, and OOD 22.5 to 28.8. The epoch-four collapse makes the intermediate state materially relevant. Its batch comparison uses the same task sets but different schedules, not randomized repeated endpoint trials. A completed update can coexist with lost function, so neither 39/40 nor 20/20 is an active-code-and-function rate. The paper's cross-model table also shows that improvement on one backend can reverse on another, but these are agent harness outcomes rather than endpoint transport measurements.

**HarnessOpt-Bench.** The seed harness, target model, environment, and verifier are pinned within each task. Development exposes cases and traces, validation returns aggregate score, and the test partition is evaluated only after nomination. A nominated candidate is scored three times per test case; the paper uses task-specific noise-resolution bands. Median optimizer use is eight evaluation calls and 82% of its case-pass allowance, illustrating that *case execution* rather than request count can dominate evaluation cost. The paper internally reports a 100-call-per-partition cap in §3.1 and a 200-invocation cap in §5.3; the four-full-case-pass cap is consistent in both passages. This discrepancy should be resolved from release artifacts or author clarification before quoting the call cap as an exact protocol value. The reported relationship between broad search and gain is observational and confounded with total edit volume; it does not isolate a mechanism. The benchmark's authors limit the current suite to Python candidates and one pinned target model per task.

## Implication for a prospective study

An adjacent [EvoHarnessBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04280) experiment helps isolate a different effect. Its 17 constructed streams progressively *add externally supplied* tools, skills, or specialist agents across three to six stages, using 802 unique tasks. In the deployment condition the system is fresh at each stage, model and prompting remain fixed, and no learned state carries over. The paper reports negative backward transfer on some earlier task cohorts simply from exposing a larger capability set. This supports checking retained behavior after a **module addition** as well as after replacement. It does not measure model-authored code changes, changes to an already loaded module, or continuous process identity. Its skill-to-task annotations are a keyword-based proxy that the authors say need not be necessary or sufficient; its headline relative percentages should not be mapped to remote-access retention.

For a benign analogue of Drexel, preserve a complete candidate ledger: proposal number, exact model output, artifact hash, gate result, accept/reject decision, active version observed by the *same* process, process birth time, retained state, and independently issued task result after each version. Score both the targeted failure and a predeclared retention suite; reserve a held-out set for final transfer checks. Report the second active-and-functional revision conditional on the first, alongside the all-starting-instances rate. The published studies motivate these measurements, but none supplies the joint rate.

[Roesner and Kohno's poisoned-benchmark study](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17817) adds a feedback-integrity qualification: selected agent descendants can carry a behavior that scores well under a compromised evaluation signal and persists through later clean evolution. Its modified-DGM case required a researcher prompt change outside the attacker's assumed control; SICA and Hyperagents mostly carried **directives**, not new executable agent code. The [separate audit](feedback-integrity-audit-2026-11-26.md) keeps those mechanisms and ten-task/three-sample denominators distinct. The result supports an independent retention check whose objective is not editable by the candidate; it does not measure Drexel's remote-access chain.

[Ruan et al.'s HARD defense-evolution study, arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.12977) adds a specific transfer warning. The model evolves a context-side policy or action-side gate rules from failure traces, immediately carrying each batch's new defense state into later *training* batches. Held-out tasks are evaluated separately. Across five long-horizon progressive-attack rounds, the gate grows from zero to 97 rules and improves its evolution-split attack-success rate from 36.6% to 23.2%, while its held-out rate moves only from 28.9% to 26.5%. A policy-only arm obtains 4.8% on that held-out attack setting but with a different benign-utility tradeoff. These are defense outcomes under a particular benchmark and single reported evaluation pass; the paper's editable objects are policies/rules and its task trajectories are not one persistent remote-access program. The contrast supports holding out a transfer set and checking collateral utility after each accepted revision, not transferring its percentages to Drexel.

[Shi et al.'s PluginRSI v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32423v2) makes *nested selection* concrete. Its fixed proposer mutates executable Python plugins in eight branches per iteration, each screened on a balanced 20-task minibatch; a recomposed harness is then tested on the full 400-task held-in SWE-bench set, and only a better full-set candidate replaces the incumbent. After 15 iterations, a selected harness is frozen for 100 held-out tasks. The reported 65% or 69% held-out resolve rate depends on the proposer/solver setting, not on repeated success of one active agent. The paper's plugin-reuse analysis then **optimizes on the previously held-out 100 tasks** as a new development set; its 69% after two reuse steps cannot be described as untouched held-out performance for that separate analysis. The result supports a tiered candidate-to-incumbent-to-final evaluation ledger, while the paper's fresh task executions and recomposed harnesses remain outside Drexel's same-process remote-access outcome. Its 15 iterations and eight branches are search budget, not two active versions in one process.

## Retained changes in fresh later sessions

[Treude and Baltes](retrospective-transfer-audit-2026-11-26.md) track 125 retrospective harness proposals from 30 coding-agent sessions through human-selected implementation and paired later-task evaluation. In their seven informative transfer events, loading or consulting a retained context file did not ensure the targeted action changed; the adaptation had to cover the particular problem encountered. Their unit is a fresh later session with instructions or known-failure files, so the counts are a transfer warning rather than a resident executable-code revision rate.

## Matched continuations after an external change

[SWE-Shift](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32196) provides a useful complementary design: its coding-agent runs fork a single saved execution prefix into Clean and Shifted continuations, then check both task completion and preservation after one task-relevant, externally imposed change. The [dedicated audit](mid-execution-change-audit-2026-11-26.md) records its 120 scenarios, nine intervention positions, 17 systems, paired and conditional measures, and resource limits. A benign Drexel analogue could similarly pair unchanged and changed continuations and require fresh evidence tied to the accepted version. SWE-Shift does not include model-authored revision, repeated changes, or remote-control access; its success rates cannot be used as Drexel rates.

## Source limits

The cited works are author-posted preprints as of this review. The RRSI legal-workspace candidate count is a selected example, SEAGym has schedule-specific runs with mixed update surfaces, and HarnessOpt-Bench uses frontier optimizer models and ephemeral Python task environments. Their sample units, selection policies, costs, and objectives differ. No arithmetic combination of their headline rates is defensible for Drexel.
