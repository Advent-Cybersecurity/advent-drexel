# Quality evidence for compact local models

**Research status, 2026-11-13 16:07 UTC.** This note audits primary evidence and specifies a prospective benign quality test. No candidate has been tested on a sealed holdout, and no result here establishes quality retention. The intended comparison is a pinned, same-source Q4_K_M GGUF reference against a smaller candidate under one fixed CPU serving policy. Resource gates are specified in the companion CPU studies.

## What the current local screen establishes

The [Qwen3-4B development screen](qwen3-4b-development-screen-2026-11-13.md) reports 16/16 final-answer correctness for each of the Bartowski Q4_K_M and Q4_K_S releases under the public `reasoned_512` prompt. Each family has eight questions, and both files produced normal stops. The stricter requested-final-line check scored the Q4_K_M file 14/16 and the Q4_K_S file 16/16, a descriptive paired difference of +12.5 percentage points for Q4_K_S on this alternate endpoint; two Q4_K_M responses appended a bare duplicate number after the answer line. The community GGUFs share a repository revision and named base, but an exact common source-weight conversion lineage has not been established. The Q4_K_S file is 113,971,200 bytes smaller, or 4.56% of the Q4_K_M file. The observed equality is a useful parser and feasibility screen for these questions. It is not a sealed estimate of composition transfer or a same-source method comparison.

The [official Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) calls this checkpoint non-thinking-only and says it does not generate `<think>` blocks. `reasoned_512` is the local prompt's name, not a vendor thinking mode. The card recommends sampling at temperature 0.7 and generally much longer outputs than our greedy, capped development probes. Accordingly, the local results measure the pinned short-response serving policy; vendor benchmark numbers under another decoding policy are not a baseline for this experiment.

The [reference raw responses](../data/generated-quality-dev-qwen3-4b-reference-2026-11-13.jsonl), SHA-256 `a07610fbf3c8a11e1c76f951f4e8885742b7c11ce81d61b784f068f5bb989284`, and [Q4_K_S raw responses](../data/generated-quality-dev-qwen3-4b-q4ks-control-2026-11-13.jsonl), SHA-256 `66098364b749fd17024654d9dda6882ad8370e0c80e815cc81665cb6bd2638a4`, show that 15/16 paired response texts differ even though their last-numeric final-answer scores match on all sixteen. Q4_K_S used 404 more completion tokens in total, a paired mean of +25.25 tokens, including +338 across the eight state cases. These are descriptive results for reused public instances, not a token-cost expectation for a new task distribution. Neither answer agreement nor text disagreement identifies the model's internal computation.

The [v4 manifest](../data/generated-quality-dev-v4-2026-11-12.json) and [generator](../scripts/run_generated_multistep_quality.py) reveal less structural variety than the four template IDs per family suggest. Arithmetic templates 0, 1, and 2 all compute `a*b + c*d - x`; template 3 computes `a*b - defective - c*d`. The four ordered-state templates all execute the same five-event dependency chain. They vary the target room, names, numbers, and whether one transfer is half or one third. These differences can test instance robustness within a known structure. They do not establish transfer to a new dependency graph. The exact scorer reads the last numeric token after a normal stop, so a correct final number does not prove that the displayed reasoning steps were correct or necessary.

For orientation only, 16 successes in 16 *independent, identically distributed* Bernoulli trials give a one-sided exact 95% lower success limit of `0.05^(1/16) = 82.9%`, or an upper failure limit of 17.1%. For one family, 8/8 gives a 68.8% lower success limit; protecting two family limits jointly at 95% by Bonferroni yields 63.1% each. These calculations do not describe unseen structures because the structural units were reused and were not sampled from a declared structure population. The two 16/16 screens also have no observed discordances, but a zero discordance count over eight cases per family does not establish a five-point noninferiority margin. Under the existing six-assertion Bonferroni construction, the conservative paired lower bound is approximately minus 49.6 percentage points in each family even with zero discordances at `n=8`. The exact arithmetic follows the [existing scorer's](../scripts/run_generated_multistep_quality.py) half-alpha bounds for the two discordance directions.

The dependence cannot be repaired by assigning a data-derived effective sample size to sixteen all-correct responses. As a sensitivity illustration only, if the three observed computation structures had instead been three independently sampled representative Bernoulli units, 3/3 would yield a one-sided 95% lower success limit of 36.8%; the arithmetic family's two structures would give 22.4%, and the ordered-state family's single structure would give 5%. These are **not confidence limits for this screen**: the structures were selected by the generator and may share the same underlying algorithm. They show how sharply the nominal 82.9% limit depends on the independent-unit assumption. There is no measured success probability for a new structure.

A group bootstrap over these old template IDs would resample only two arithmetic forms and one state graph. Since every observed response is correct, each resample would still report 100% and a zero-width empirical interval. That interval would reflect the absence of observed within-support variation, not uncertainty over unobserved structures. The observed point rates of 100% and zero observed paired regression remain worth reporting, with this structural limitation attached.

The earlier 1.5B Q4_K_M reference scored 5/8 arithmetic and 3/8 ordered state on this same public manifest after a prompt and token-budget revision. This is a separate base and conversion lineage, so the difference with the 4B result is a development observation rather than a controlled scale ablation. The [old protocol](generated-multistep-quality-protocol-2026-11-12.md) correctly did not freeze an evaluation manifest for that weaker reference.

## Prospective structural holdout

This is the preregistration target for the next candidate comparison. It is not yet a frozen manifest or a scored experiment.

1. **Freeze the artifacts and task contract.** Record the official BF16 checkpoint revision and file hashes, the exact intermediate full-precision GGUF, imatrix digest if one is used, per-tensor quantization map, converter build and flags, architecture, `llama.cpp` commit, Q4_K_M reference export, candidate export, tokenizer, chat template, sampler, prompt, context, output cap, `--no-repack` or repack setting, per-request prefix-cache behavior, and server RAM-cache size. Export both GGUFs from the same pinned source with a documented conversion recipe and verify the artifact hashes; re-derive the files bit-for-bit where practical. For a physically pruned candidate, also pin recovery data, training code, and budget before any sealed query. A third-party community Q4_K_M is a provisional reference until this control exists.
2. **Define two benign families with a generated oracle.** Inventory tasks combine grouped quantities, additions, losses, and conditional updates. Ordered-state tasks move quantities among named stores and ask for a final state after dependent events. Build a compositional grammar rather than four sentence templates. Each task records its primitive operators, input constants, event order, query-ancestor graph, distractor nodes, maximum dependency depth, oracle trace, and final answer. Include branch, merge, overwrite, and delayed-reference structures with multiple valid surface phrasings. The [iGSM generator's explicit structure and dependency graphs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/iGSM/tree/a1ed1d04600add811beb08b58d9912ed30999642) offer an implementation example, although its graph construction is not a validation of our proposed split. The oracle should be independently checked against a second implementation on development structures before freeze.
3. **Separate structures semantically.** Assign a canonical graph ID after removing names and numeric values, normalizing commutative children, and retaining operator labels, edge direction, update order, and queried state. Check whether two nominally different graphs implement the same end-to-end function over the declared input domain. A finite set of probe inputs can flag possible equivalence but cannot prove its absence; use symbolic equivalence or exhaustive bounded-domain checking where the restricted grammar permits it. Put unresolved possible-equivalence pairs in the same conservative exclusion class until resolved. Keep all structures or equivalence classes seen in public development, recovery training, or model selection out of the holdout. Avoid counting distractor-only variations as new query-ancestor structures. Ma and Anand's observational-irreducibility condition cannot be imported as a literal guarantee for one question with one target answer: a constant rule can fit that single input-output observation. The event-deletion and sensitivity filters rule out specified local shortcuts only. A secondary panel of multiple numeric instances per blueprint can test within-structure consistency, with the whole blueprint kept as one uncertainty cluster. Even then, success does not reveal an unobserved internal algorithm.
4. **Sample new structure clusters before candidate scoring.** The primary sampling unit is an independently generated task blueprint containing one canonical query-ancestor structure and one prespecified primary instance. Draw blueprints independently from a fixed grammar mixture with recorded seed and rejection rules. Before drawing the sealed ledger, estimate the grammar's post-exclusion collision probability by enumerating its canonical support or simulating independent draws. For `n` draws with graph probabilities `p_g`, the expected number of matching graph pairs is `n(n-1)/2` times `sum_g p_g^2`, and the expected number of unique graph IDs is `sum_g [1-(1-p_g)^n]`; report both. A small grammar can produce thousands of independent instances yet still test only a few structural algorithms. Rejection for development or recovery overlap must be applied independently to each draw under the same rule; retain any duplicate or functionally equivalent IDs within the sealed sample and report their frequency. Do not delete within-holdout collisions after the draw. Additional numeric and wording variants within each blueprint form a secondary robustness panel and never inflate the primary denominator. Predeclare a mixture of at least two query-dependency-depth strata in each family, such as depth 3 to 4 and depth 5 to 7, and draw the stratum label independently for each blueprint. Draw within each stratum using frozen minimum ancestor-event counts and rejection rules; record attempted draws and rejection reasons. If branch balance is required, draw branch as another prespecified stratum with known mixture weights and use the corresponding stratified estimator, or accept the realized random branch mix without changing the sample. Choose mixture weights and total count using reference-only development and prospective power simulation. Fixed quotas or deduplication within the sealed sample would change the sampling design and require a corresponding stratified or finite-population analysis. Freeze and hash the entire structure ledger, case manifest, oracle code, parser, scoring policy, analysis code, and sample count before either sealed arm runs. The structure ledger must show zero canonical and known functional-equivalence overlap with development and recovery sets.
5. **Use one objective primary endpoint.** A normal-stop response is correct only if the frozen parser extracts the oracle final answer exactly. Transport failure, missing answer, and output-cap stop are incorrect in the intention-to-test denominator. The parser must be tested against synthetic strings and reference-only development responses before freeze. If the last-numeric-token rule is retained, audit development responses where it extracts an intermediate or contradicted value; report strict-final-line agreement as a sensitivity check. Choosing a stricter rule prospectively changes the task contract and requires a fresh reference development screen. For stateful executable tasks, check required final environment state and task-critical effect invariants while allowing any path that satisfies those requirements. No manual repair or after-the-fact parser adjustment is allowed for the primary score.
6. **Control order and leakage.** Run arms on identical case IDs and serving settings. Pin the model bytes, GGUF metadata and chat template, runtime binary, launch flags, generation settings, timeout, per-request `cache_prompt` value, and `--cache-ram` setting. Hash the exact rendered prompt and token IDs for each case, including system text and any date or other dynamic field, and verify that paired arms received the intended same task and prompt. Record case order, process and block IDs, prior request history within each block, and reported cached prompt tokens because the request hash alone does not fix cache state. A clean confirmatory serving policy can set `--no-cache-prompt --cache-ram 0` for both arms, provided a public reference pilot first confirms that it works and the resource measurements are repeated under those same flags. If deployment-default caching is the primary target instead, freeze a shared case order and fresh-process block plan, then report a separately planned order and restart sensitivity. If whole-suite model order could drift with thermal or system load, balance fresh-process blocks or alternate arm order while retaining the same paired task ledger. Hash raw responses and server logs. Keep holdout questions, gold, and responses out of prompts, recovery training, candidate selection, and public issue discussions. Do not inspect either sealed arm's outcomes to revise the grammar, case selection, parser, or candidate before the paired analysis is complete. Any candidate query against this ledger consumes that holdout for that candidate family. If results lead to a model or parser revision, evaluate the revision on a newly generated and separately frozen ledger.

The local test can support a claim about the declared generated distribution and the frozen pair. It cannot prove absence of unknown pretraining exposure or parity with a frontier model. Generating tasks after the base checkpoint release and checking exact text overlap reduce exact-instance leakage risk; they do not rule out prior knowledge of the same algorithm or story schema. A new, independent family such as audited AgentCoMa items or [BFCL V3 multi-turn state checks](https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/blogs/13_bfcl_v3_multi_turn.html) can test a different transfer surface, but its public status and checker behavior must be reported.

For scale, a hypothetical uniform grammar with only 100 eligible canonical graph IDs would yield about 100 unique IDs and 7,194 matching-ID pairs in 1,200 independent draws. The item-level binomial gate would still estimate performance on that 100-graph mixture if draws were genuinely independent, but it could not justify transfer to graph types outside that support. This is an algebraic illustration, not a measured count for the unbuilt grammar. The pre-seal support audit should reject a grammar whose collision rate defeats the intended structural-diversity claim, before any candidate result exists.

Before drawing the sealed ledger, have two independent reviewers solve a separate public development sample of generated questions from the displayed wording without seeing the oracle trace. Put every reviewed case and its canonical graph ID in the development exclusion list. Reconcile divergent interpretations against the grammar, revise or reject ambiguous templates under a recorded rule, and rerun the oracle checks. Record the audit denominator, reviewer flags, disagreements, and repair decisions. If a quantitative defect-rate check is needed, a separate simple random audit of 72 independently drawn questions per family with zero reviewer-detected ambiguities would put a simultaneous one-sided 95% upper bound below 5% on each family's *detected* ambiguity rate, using Bonferroni across the two families. That calculation assumes stable review sensitivity and does not bound ambiguity missed by both reviewers; any grammar repair requires a new audit sample. Freeze the revised grammar and exclusion list, then draw and freeze the sealed ledger under the declared rejection rule. Once either sealed arm is scored, an ambiguity discovered from its response is a reported test defect, not grounds for removing that item from the primary denominator or changing its gold after the fact.

## Primary estimates and pass rule

For each family, let `R_i` and `C_i` be the binary final success indicators for the same primary blueprint draw `i`. Publish `n`, reference and candidate rates, `both correct`, `reference only`, `candidate only`, and `neither`, plus the paired difference `mean(C_i - R_i)`. The primary estimand weights independent blueprint draws equally under the frozen grammar mixture; a graph ID drawn twice receives its mixture frequency, not equal weight per distinct ID. Numeric or paraphrase variants are secondary and are summarized by blueprint, not as independent trials. Report counts for each length stratum and the paired rate difference there, even if those strata are underpowered. A claim specifically about long chains needs a separately powered and multiplicity-controlled long-chain gate.

For the secondary variant panel, resample whole blueprint clusters with replacement, carrying both model outcomes and every numeric or wording variant together, and recompute the prespecified cluster-weighted paired difference. If the sample was stratified, resample clusters within stratum and combine them with the frozen target-mixture weights. Publish the number of distinct blueprint IDs and canonical graph IDs, the collision rate, and percentile interval alongside the raw point estimate. Treat the cluster bootstrap as a sensitivity analysis: with few distinct structures or all-success clusters, it can yield a deceptively narrow or zero-width interval and cannot certify transfer to missing graph types.

For an optional behavioral sensitivity check, freeze oracle-verified counterfactual variants before either arm runs: one changes a query-ancestor event so the correct endpoint changes, and, where a genuine distractor exists, one changes only that distractor so the correct endpoint stays fixed. Score the original and variants as one cluster, and report the fraction of eligible blueprints on which each arm gets every endpoint right. This tests whether outputs respect specified changes in the visible task. It cannot establish which internal computation produced an answer, and the related variants do not add independent primary trials.

Retain the [existing six-assertion rule](generated-multistep-quality-protocol-2026-11-12.md) for a two-family, one-primary-instance-per-blueprint comparison: in each family, a one-sided 95% simultaneous lower bound must put both reference and candidate success above 60%, and candidate minus reference above minus five percentage points. Allocate alpha `0.05/6` to each assertion. For the paired bound, use exact lower and upper Clopper-Pearson limits on the candidate-only and reference-only rates at half that alpha, then subtract. This is conservative but valid for independent, identically distributed primary draws from the declared grammar mixture when the pinned serving process does not add cross-case outcome dependence. Repeated structure IDs from independent draws are part of that mixture and must be disclosed; a large collision rate would narrow the population represented and weaken the intended unseen-structure claim. A shared outage, mutable server state, or run-block timeout pattern could correlate the deployment-oriented success outcomes despite independent questions. Record block IDs and infrastructure errors, and prespecify a block-level sensitivity; if broad run dependence is evident, withhold the item-level exact coverage claim. If fixed stratum quotas, adaptive case selection, or related variants enter the primary denominator, this exact-binomial claim is unavailable. Use a prespecified cluster or stratified analysis and describe its approximation and target population; do not silently apply item-level exact intervals to correlated variants.

This rule establishes noninferiority within five points for the declared task distribution if it passes. It does not establish statistical equivalence, which would also require an upper bound below a positive margin. Its 60% absolute floor is a minimum competence check, not evidence of broad or frontier-level capability. A separate claim about a difficult subgroup, such as long chains, requires its own prospectively powered bound. Report descriptive two-sided paired intervals as well as the decision bounds so a failed gate can be distinguished from a clearly unfavorable point estimate. Any superiority, equivalence, or subgroup claim needs a prespecified testing family and error allocation; it cannot be inferred from a failed lower-bound gate.

The primary contrast is against Q4_K_M, which is already a compressed checkpoint. A pass would not quantify loss from the official BF16 source. If the intended claim includes preservation of source-checkpoint quality, run BF16 on the same sealed blueprint ledger under a fixed feasible serving policy and allocate error for that additional contrast before exposure. If BF16 cannot be run, state the comparison as candidate versus Q4_K_M only.

One fixed pair of GGUF files supports an artifact-level claim for those bytes and that serving policy. It does not estimate the average effect of a quantization or recovery *method* across calibration samples, training seeds, converter revisions, or separately exported artifacts. A method-level claim would require independently produced artifact pairs under a declared production process, with artifact as an additional uncertainty unit and a prospective analysis across both artifacts and task blueprints. The sealed task ledger should not be recycled for adaptive artifact selection.

The exact-component construction prioritizes transparent coverage over width. [Newcombe's paired-proportion study](https://www.site.uottawa.ca/~nat/Courses/csi5388/Newcombe.1998.pdf) shows why an ordinary Wald interval or an exact interval conditional only on the number of discordant pairs can behave poorly for a *risk difference*. [Hsueh et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11414572/) propose unconditional exact paired-endpoint tests for equivalence and noninferiority; [Lloyd and Moldovan](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18314932/) develop a more powerful exact unconditional matched-pair noninferiority test in their numerical study. These are methodological candidates, not analyses already implemented here. A paired score or unconditional method could be more efficient, but changing the gate requires a fully specified implementation, coverage checks, and a new prospective sample-size calculation before any sealed response. Do not substitute an easier interval after observing a near miss.

The conservative gate cannot pass at any size below **107 independent primary blueprint draws per family**, even in the best possible case where every pair is correct: with zero candidate-only and reference-only discordances, its paired lower limit is `-[1-(0.05/12)^(1/n)]`, which crosses minus five points only at `n=107`. Draws may repeat a canonical graph ID under the frozen mixture, so 107 draws do not guarantee 107 distinct structures. This is a mathematical minimum for the frozen six-assertion construction, not an adequate sample-size recommendation. Nonzero discordances and less than perfect accuracy require more cases. The local CPU cost of hundreds or thousands of full responses must be estimated before promising a confirmatory run.

Before generating the sealed ledger, choose `n` using a reference-only structural development pilot and simulation of the complete six-assertion rule across plausible paired outcome tables. The reference-only pilot can inform the reference floor and per-response cost; it **cannot estimate candidate accuracy or the paired discordance rate**, which strongly controls noninferiority power. The old [planning exercise](../data/paired-quality-sample-planning-2026-11-12.json) assumes independent items, 80% true success, and 10% total symmetric discordance; in 10,000 simulations per scenario it passed 187 times at 300 items per family and 9,131 times at 1,200, or 1.87% and 91.31%. At 20% total discordance with the same 80% equal arm rates, the 1,200-per-family scenario passed 3,705 of 10,000 times, or 37.05%. Binomial Monte Carlo standard errors for the two 1,200-case pass fractions are approximately 0.28 and 0.48 percentage points; these quantify simulation noise under the assumed tables, not uncertainty about the actual pair. These are scenario calculations, not fitted power for the 4B pair. A fixed small run can be valuable as an exploratory pilot, but a failed conservative gate at low `n` is inconclusive. Do not enlarge the sealed sample after looking at candidate outcomes unless a sequential design and error spending were frozen in advance.

If there are multiple candidate methods or controls, choose one primary candidate versus Q4_K_M before sealing. For a composition-conditioned pruning hypothesis, predeclare the same-width ordinary-pruning and near-byte bit-only controls with matched source and recovery budget. Report each paired comparison and its interval. Any claim that the custom selector outperforms both controls requires separate multiplicity allocation and adequate power. A Q4_K_S comparison from the current community releases remains a development control until same-source exports exist.

At the V2 pilot's observed 44.22-second mean warm reference response time, 1,200 primary cases in each of two families would consume about 29.5 hours for 2,400 reference responses alone. If a candidate took the same time, both arms would require about 59 hours of sequential response time, before model loads, failures, restarts, or secondary probes. V3's twelve arithmetic cases averaged 59.83 seconds each, and V4's deeper state-only panel averaged 82.33 seconds each. Applying those two observed cell means to 1,200 cases in each family gives about 47.4 hours for the reference and 94.8 hours for two equally fast arms. The future grammar mixture, structure lengths, and candidate latency are unknown. These extrapolations are scheduling sensitivities, not runtime guarantees or candidate speed estimates.

## Failure analysis and reporting contract

The primary denominator includes every scheduled case ID in each arm. Preserve raw response, finish reason, parser extraction, oracle answer, model hash, prompt hash, token counts, task timing, and infrastructure error. Publish the two-by-two paired outcome table for each family and length stratum. Distinguish model answer error from service failure in diagnostics, while counting both as unsuccessful in the deployment-oriented primary score. This endpoint estimates success of the pinned served system under the run conditions; a difference caused by crashes or timeouts cannot be called a difference in model reasoning. Before sealing, specify one bounded, identical transport-only retry rule, if any, and retain all attempts. The existing protocol instead records a failed request as incorrect, with no retry. If broad infrastructure faults make the scheduled paired run incomplete or noncomparable, preserve the records and withhold a confirmatory model-quality claim. Report an infrastructure-only sensitivity analysis without using it to replace the primary result; complete-case exclusion after seeing which arm failed would select on the outcome.

On a blinded sample of errors and successes, tag observable output features: no extractable answer, cap stop, wrong referent or target state, arithmetic mismatch, incorrect event order, ignored conditional update, omitted dependency, distractor use, and displayed-step inconsistency. Report tag definitions, inter-rater agreement if humans label them, and examples from development or a separately shareable audit set. A displayed trace is evidence about the written explanation, not direct evidence of the model's internal reasoning. Count answers that are final-correct with an inconsistent trace, and final-wrong with a correct intermediate value, without changing the primary score. Record per-task generation length and total served-task CPU-seconds so a lower token cost is not mistaken for lower cost per successful task.

For the optional two-pass diagnostic, ask each primitive question in a separate prompt containing the original task, and score the complete task in a separate fresh context. Use fixed meaning-preserving paraphrases of each atomic probe and separately check aligned subquestions before describing a full-task error as a residual composition failure. Report `P(full task wrong | all scored primitives correct)` for each arm, the number eligible after each gate, the unconditional full-task rate, and the overlap and common intersection of eligible sets. Audit a random sample of primitive judgments and paraphrases for false positives and meaning drift. Because eligibility itself changes by model, an apparent reduction in the conditional gap need not mean improved full-task quality. Any structural-hint intervention belongs in development or a separately frozen secondary experiment; it cannot alter the primary prompt after candidate results are seen.

The final report should contain point estimates and simultaneous intervals, exact frozen hashes, all exclusions or errors, negative and null findings, and scope restrictions. If the candidate fails a floor or the interval crosses the noninferiority margin, state that the preset gate was not met; a null or underpowered result is not proof of equivalence or inferiority. A favorable public development score or a zero observed regression on reused templates cannot replace the sealed structural result.

## Immediate disposition

Keep the 16-item screen, all three 24-item reference pilots, and the twelve-item V4 reference pilot as public development material. V2 removes cap stops on its reused authored cases and exposes a normal-stop arithmetic state error plus a final-line parser gap. V3's new event programs also score 23/24, with one clear missed reserve update and no cap stops at 1,536 tokens. The V3 support and dependency audits show why distinct full-program IDs and seven listed events do not guarantee distinct, deep query-relevant work. V4 fixes the shallow-path defect in the state development cell, scores 10/12, and exposes two wrong endpoint answers plus one additional erroneous displayed trace hidden by a correct endpoint. These pilots inform grammar filtering, prompt, cap, parser, and time planning; they do not estimate candidate noninferiority or sealed unseen-structure performance.
