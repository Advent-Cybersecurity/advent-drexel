# Diagnostic context and repair success in DDBench

26 November 2026. Source-only review of [Yan et al., *DDBench*, arXiv version 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14863). The question for Drexel is whether a future, benign revision study should provide a candidate editor with observed failure state rather than only a symptom. This paper studies code repair in isolated distributed-system repositories. It does not test Drexel, malicious code, or a resident remote-access process.

## What was compared

The authors curated 60 historical bugs in 13 open-source distributed systems. Thirty-one tier-1 cases demand reasoning about distributed protocol state or concurrency; 15 tier-2 cases span multiple processes; 14 tier-3 cases localize within one process. For every case, the same mini-swe-agent scaffold and model received either a symptom and repository, or those materials plus a bounded diagnostic bundle. The bundles contain one to three short Markdown files (median 321 estimated tokens, maximum 1,403), generated by Claude Code/Opus 4.6 and reviewed. They mix code-investigation notes, test/crash output, and less often runtime state or traces. The paper did not ablate these types separately. Each model–case–condition yields a final patch judged by a hidden PASS/FAIL oracle. The case runs are separate hermetic containers; cluster nodes, when needed, are processes communicating over loopback inside a container. Ten models, including self-hosted 31B and 120B entries, faced limits of 450 agent steps, $5 in token cost, and 30 minutes per case. [Dataset and setup](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14863).

On the tier-1 set, the aggregate pass rate across 10 models and 31 cases rose from **31.0% to 50.0%** with the bundle, a **19.0 percentage-point** difference over **310 paired model–case comparisons**. The pair states were 72 failures converted to passes, 13 passes converted to failures, and 225 unchanged. Across tiers 2 and 3, the corresponding reported means rose 48.0% to 62.7% and 67.9% to 77.1%. These are controlled comparisons for this curated benchmark and scaffold. The 310 pairs reuse cases and models, so they are not 310 independent systems or Drexel attempts. [Main outcomes](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14863).

## Why the feedback signal needs a causal check

The paper gives cases where a runtime-only state snapshot or goroutine dump helps the agent find the root cause. It also gives a counterexample: in Kvrocks-3356, the supplied reproducer output showed excessive write-ahead-log growth, while the underlying fault involved a blocked replication feed thread. Two models that passed without the bundle failed with it. The authors caution that the symptom-only passing patches cleared the oracle threshold narrowly and did not implement the upstream fix; that makes this a strong illustration of **anchoring in the observed trajectories**, but a weaker claim about durable correctness. Diagnostic context is useful only insofar as it helps identify the causal fault and supports independent function checks. [Case studies](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14863).

The reported difference cannot be transferred numerically to Drexel. The study uses one scaffold, one generator for the diagnostic bundles, a single curated release, no content-level bundle ablation, and repository patch oracles rather than accepted versions of a running component. Its self-hosted models are large and do not establish a small, CPU-only resident budget. The work does not observe two revisions adopted by one continuing process or a remote-control result after each. [Limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14863).

## Implication for the paper and a later benign protocol

Treat the quality and causal relevance of feedback as a separate feasibility variable. A later authorized benign study should preserve the exact symptom, diagnostic artifact, candidate patch, and independent result for **each** attempted and accepted revision. It should distinguish whether context improved repair of the triggering fault from whether a candidate merely passed a narrow oracle or retained earlier functionality. DDBench supports testing that distinction; it supplies no probability for Drexel's joined sequence. No model, code, or sample was executed in this review.
